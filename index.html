ProShop 101

What ProShop is, in one minute

ProShop is an all-in-one, paperless operating system for precision machine shops. It replaces spreadsheets, whiteboards, paper travelers, and tribal knowledge with one connected system — where the quote becomes the work order becomes the inspection record becomes the invoice, with no duplicate entry. It's really three products in one:

ERP
Enterprise Resource Planning

The business backbone — quoting, orders, purchasing, inventory, scheduling, costing, invoicing. The record of what the shop is doing and what it costs.

MES
Manufacturing Execution System

The shop-floor layer — live work-order status, time tracking, scheduling, work instructions. The record of how the work is running right now.

QMS
Quality Management System

The compliance layer — inspection, calibration, cert packets, non-conformance, audits. The record of proving the parts meet spec.

How They Relate

Four things they all share

ProShop's whole value is that these roles share one database. These records only make sense once you see several personas depending on them — one person sets or contributes, several depend on the result.

Customer Contact page
Set once by QA & Order Entry, read everywhere

Flags set once (FAI, CFC, form format, terms) silently flow into the cert packet, the AS9102 form, and the invoice. A missed flag surfaces as a rejected shipment downstream.

Cert packet
Everyone contributes, one packet ships

Vendor certs (Receiver), material specs (Planner), inspection results (Operator + QA), requirement flags (Order Entry) — all converge into one packet. Any missing piece and the customer's dock rejects the box.

On-time delivery
Cascaded upstream, measured downstream

Not one KPI but a cascade: the Buyer holds vendors to 97% so the Planner can hit the shop's 95% so the customer counts the delivery on time.

Dashboards
Same database, nine filtered views

Customer service, Project assignment, Scheduling, Purchasing, Receiving, Work cell, Inspection, Inventory control, Shipping — each is a filtered view of the same work orders. Change one, everyone downstream sees it.

Company Context

By machinists, for machinists

ProShop wasn't built by a software company that discovered manufacturing — it was built by machinists who got tired of running a shop on spreadsheets. That origin is why the product fits the way it does.

1997
Pro CNC machine shop founded
2000
Outgrew Excel; began building ProShop
2008

2014

2016

2026

Ideal Customer

Who buys ProShop

Deliberately targeted at high-stakes machine shops — not “anyone with a machine,” but shops where a mistake could be catastrophic, and growing mid-market shops where tribal knowledge is starting to break the business.

BULLSEYE
Industry: Aerospace, Defense, Medical
Size: 20–75 employees
Compliance: AS9100, ITAR, CMMC, ISO 13485 (mandatory)
Pain: “We lose contracts if we fail audits.”
Buys because: Risk mitigation & survival
STRONG FIT
Industry: Industrial, Auto, Robotics, Energy, Oil & Gas
Size: 10–19 or 76–150 employees
Compliance: ISO 9001, or aspiring to bullseye certs
Pain: “Manual errors are killing our margin.”
Buys because: Scaling & professionalization
